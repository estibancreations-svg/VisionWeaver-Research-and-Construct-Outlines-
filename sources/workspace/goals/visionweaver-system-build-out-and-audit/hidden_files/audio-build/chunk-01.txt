VisionWeaver Directives and System Review The operating directives that teach the system how stories are created here, a critical comparison of everything saved to date, and a rebuild outline — recommendation only. Verdict, up front. What is saved today is not a production system. It is four user-interface lineages, a set of documents, and a working method that lives in conversation. The stories, packages, boards, thumbnails, and audio produced this month were made by following the directives in this document — in chat, by hand — not by any stage executing inside VisionWeaver. Until one system is declared canonical and the stage chain below is built as data and checks, every new “version” will make the problem worse, not better. This document does three jobs. Part A states the 16 directives exactly as they were directed, each with how to create it, the return it must produce, and the gate that stops the next stage. Part B compares the saved systems and names the contradictions. Part C outlines the rebuild and why, with the decisions only you can make listed separately. Nothing in Part C has been started. Scope of the audit behind Part B: MASTER_CEO_DASHBOARD (v3 workspace), VisionWeaver, VisionWeaver-Prime, Director Studio builds, Crossroads-of-Identity, This-Is-Your-Life, The-Arc, the local archive, and the Supabase wiring as the repository files state it. Read-only. Nothing was changed, moved, disconnected, or merged. How to read the directives A directive is not advice. It is a programming instruction for the system: a rule, a procedure that produces it, a named return, and a gate. If a stage cannot produce its required return, the stage did not happen — no matter what a screen shows. THE STAGE CHAIN (DIRECTIVE 12, IN ONE LINE) Outline → script draft → your approval → production package → thumbnails (text-verified) → character and scene boards → guide audio (verbatim, content-checked) → render (funding-gated) → QC preview → publish. Every arrow is a gate. Skipping an arrow is how the failures in Part B happened. WHAT “REQUIRED RETURN” MEANS IN PRODUCTION Artifact: a file or record that can be opened later, not a claim in chat. Verification: the artifact read back and checked against its source — script against audio, thumbnail file against its text, board against its spec. Open-decisions list: every package carries what is decided, what is not, and what is blocked. An unconfirmed pick is labeled unconfirmed, never presented as settled. Part A — Directives D1–D2 D1 — One track at a time Directive: One project per track. Never mix projects, chats, accounts, counties, or funding tracks on assumption. Business stays in its chat; travel and scheduling stay separate. How to create it: Open a track with a named goal/workspace and a named lead. Every file, board, and package carries the track name. Cross-track references are explicit links, never merges. Required return: A track sheet naming the track, its lead, its workspace, and its open decisions. Gate: No work starts on a second track inside the first track's thread. D2 — Hold-then-go Directive: A hold means proposal only. Nothing is built, rendered, posted, disconnected, moved, or spent on a hold. Work resumes only on your explicit go. How to create it: When you say hold or wait, write the proposal and checklist and stop. State plainly who holds the ball. When a part finishes, bring the next part forward immediately — you never guess whether you are waiting or the system is working. Required return: The checklist itself, with the hold marked and the next step named. Gate: No build, render, post, spend, disconnect, or move without an explicit go for that step. Part A — Directives D3–D4 D3 — Originality lock (IP) Directive: Original characters, lines, titles, descriptions, tags, thumbnails, and branding only. Banned anywhere: Yoda, Grogu, Jedi, the Force, lightsaber, Star Wars, “This is the Way,” and any borrowed franchise names, terms, or lore. Epic feel is allowed; borrowed identity is not. How to create it: Write from the series' own cosmology — here, “the Current.” Run a banned-term scan over script, package, thumbnails, and posting copy before any publish step. Record unpicked variants as saved variants (for example, “mirrored sisters”); never discard them. Required return: A clean scan result (zero matches) plus a counsel-review line item, still open until counsel reviews name, design, voice, and the first two scripts. Gate: No publish without a clean scan. Counsel review remains open, never assumed done. D4 — Script spine Directive: Every teaching follows hook (under 8 seconds) → story → reframe → practice → payoff/bridge, with the payoff at 70–85% of runtime. Titles name the lesson, never the character. The script is the source: its words do not change in production. Narration is recorded in chunks of 30 seconds or less. How to create it: Draft from the outline in the lead's mode (Elder / Guinan / Spock / Picard). Re-point settings to the locked leads. Keep the telling personal yet vague enough that it is not finite or direct — anyone must be able to stand in it. Required return: The full script text, verbatim, with mode, lead, and setting stated.
