# VisionWeaver Directives — DRAFT (from the user's direction history, 2026-10-27)
Working draft. Final document to be completed after the read-only repo audit returns.
Each directive has: STATEMENT (the rule), HOW TO CREATE IT (the procedure that produces it),
REQUIRED RETURN (the artifact/evidence that stage must hand back), GATE (what blocks progress).

## D1. One track at a time
- STATEMENT: One project per track. Never mix projects, chats, accounts, counties, or funding tracks on assumption. Business stays in its chat; travel/scheduling stay separate.
- HOW TO CREATE IT: Open a track with a named goal/workspace and a named lead. Every file, board, and package carries the track's name. Cross-track references are explicit links, never merges.
- REQUIRED RETURN: A track sheet naming the track, its lead, its workspace, and its open decisions.
- GATE: No work starts on a second track inside the first track's thread.

## D2. Hold-then-go
- STATEMENT: A hold means proposal only. Nothing is built, rendered, posted, disconnected, moved, or spent on a hold. Work resumes only on the user's explicit go.
- HOW TO CREATE IT: When the user says hold/wait, write the proposal + checklist and stop. State plainly who holds the ball. When a part finishes, bring the next part forward immediately — the user never has to guess whether he is waiting or the system is working.
- REQUIRED RETURN: The checklist itself, with the hold marked and the next step named.
- GATE: No build, render, post, spend, disconnect, or move without an explicit go for that step.

## D3. Originality lock (IP)
- STATEMENT: Original characters, lines, titles, descriptions, tags, thumbnails, and branding only. Banned anywhere in the system: Yoda, Grogu, Jedi, the Force, lightsaber, Star Wars, "This is the Way," and any borrowed franchise names, terms, or lore. Epic feel is allowed; borrowed identity is not.
- HOW TO CREATE IT: Write from the series' own cosmology (here: "the Current"). Run a banned-term scan over script, package, thumbnails, and posting copy before any publish step. Record unpicked variants as saved variants (e.g. "mirrored sisters"), never discard them.
- REQUIRED RETURN: A clean scan result (zero matches) plus a counsel-review line item, still open until counsel reviews name, design, voice, and first two scripts.
- GATE: No publish without a clean scan. Counsel review remains an open item, never assumed done.

## D4. Script spine
- STATEMENT: Every teaching follows hook (<8 sec) → story → reframe → practice → payoff/bridge, with the payoff at 70–85% of runtime. Titles name the lesson, never the character. The script is the source: its words do not change in production. Narration is recorded in chunks of 30 seconds or less.
- HOW TO CREATE IT: Draft from the outline in the lead's mode (Elder / Guinan / Spock / Picard). Re-point settings to the locked leads. Keep the telling personal yet vague enough that it is not finite or direct — anyone must be able to stand in it.
- REQUIRED RETURN: The full script text, verbatim, with mode, lead, and setting stated.
- GATE: No production package, thumbnail, board, or audio until the script draft exists; no render until the user approves the script.

## D5. Character continuity — the board system
- STATEMENT: No character, scene, room, or angle ships without a board. Every character board carries: turnaround (front/side/back) + 360 strip, variation study, close details, spec sheet, anchor name, likeness ladder with correction callouts, kinesic callouts, and body-type anchor. Both locked variants of a character (e.g. glasses and non-glasses) are boarded separately; a variant is never "fixed."
- HOW TO CREATE IT: Generate from the locked portrait reference. Before saving any generated frame, ask the retention question: "Should this image be referred to as [ANCHOR NAME]?" Record drift at save time, not at render.
- REQUIRED RETURN: The board image(s) plus the written board (spec sheet, ladder, callouts).
- GATE: A character with no board cannot enter a scene board, a thumbnail, or a render.

## D6. Environment standard
- STATEMENT: Every room/setting board carries four panels: Wide Establishing, Medium Texture, Key Prop/Detail, Lighting Reference — plus multi-angle coverage of the same room and a floor plan with blocking.
- HOW TO CREATE IT: Build the set board first, then scene boards that reuse only boarded angles. A position change in a setting must already exist on a board.
- REQUIRED RETURN: The set board image + floor plan + angle list.
- GATE: No scene ships in an unboarded room or from an unboarded angle.

## D7. Prompt standard
- STATEMENT: Every board ships with two machine forms: Type 1, a Master JSON Payload (visual standard: aspect ratio, color science, dynamic range, frame rate, audio routing), and Type 2, a Markdown System Directive (art logic: physics rendering, kinesic detail, transition lock).
- HOW TO CREATE IT: Attach both forms as the header of every character and scene board. New example batches are absorbed into the template; locked boards are never rebuilt to absorb them.
- REQUIRED RETURN: The two forms, present on the board, with transition locks recorded per beat in the shotdeck (Linear Snap / Dot 0.5s / True Black, or as re-picked).
- GATE: A board without its prompt header does not enter production.

## D8. Crossroads production standard
- STATEMENT: The Crossroads of Identity production standards govern this series and everything after it: pipeline order (Avatar Engineering → Worlds & Locations → Books & script → Locks & maps → Cast & avatars → Shot bible → Pictures → Motion → Narration booth → Sound & music → Edit → Deliver → Publish & social); visual change every 8–15 sec; pacing reset every 30–60 sec; hook <8 sec with no branding opener; payoff at 70–85%; music 9–12 dB under voice; Whisper-class ASR captions checked against the script; Gen-4.5/Act-Two-class render/QC; budget as cost per finished second; preview → verify → publish. Finished pieces ship clean — no raw production note burned into any frame.
- HOW TO CREATE IT: Map each standard to the episode concretely in the production package (not as a generic list). Record render class and cost per finished second before rendering.
- REQUIRED RETURN: The standards-mapping page of the production package, with every standard mapped to a concrete choice.
- GATE: No publish from an unverified render. No publish with a production note in frame.

## D9. Delivery format
- STATEMENT: Print first, then the audio of exactly that, verbatim, right after. No synopsis plus a separate summary voice note — that pairing reads as repetition. Otherwise voice notes stay SHORT (under ~1 minute), one pass, essentials only, never a full re-read, never duplicate recordings of the same text. Written replies stay tight; recaps use checklists. Analysis is deep, finite, and definitive; thorough over fast.
- HOW TO CREATE IT: Write the piece, then read that piece — not a summary of it. Verify each file exists before sending. Check each file's duration against its text length before shipping.
- REQUIRED RETURN: The printed piece + the matching audio file, verified present, with duration checked.
- GATE: No audio ships on a duration check alone — content must be checked against its script (see D12).

## D10. Funding gate
- STATEMENT: Billing stays on hold until funding comes in. No paid render, paid tool, or paid work starts on assumption. Free work only until the user says otherwise.
- HOW TO CREATE IT: Plan every sprint in free-only form first (writing, images via free generation, documents, boards, guide audio). Mark every render/publish step as funding-gated.
- REQUIRED RETURN: A sprint plan that states what is free and what is gated, with no gated step started.
- GATE: No render, no paid generation, no publish spend without the user's funding go-ahead.

## D11. Weekly engine and posting
- STATEMENT: Weekly cadence: one 6–12 min teaching + one 45–60 sec Short (single aphorism + practice), automated in VisionWeaver. Every episode ships a posting package: exact long title, Short beat sheet, two-paragraph description, exact safe tags, channels (YouTube Estiban Creations / Instagram Master_Your_Thinking), weekly calendar, posting checklist, and IP checklist. No posting day/time is invented.
- HOW TO CREATE IT: Build the posting package as the last page(s) of the production package. Still-image posts (thumbnails, quote cards) may go live without a render; video waits for render.
- REQUIRED RETURN: The posting package + text-verified thumbnails (main 16:9 + Short cover), each with its text read back and confirmed exact.
- GATE: No video post without render + QC preview pass. No post with an unverified thumbnail text.

## D12. Approval gates and required returns (the chain)
- STATEMENT: Each stage returns a named artifact before the next stage starts. The chain: outline → script draft → user approval → production package (script verbatim + standards mapping + scene map + posting package) → thumbnails (text-verified) → character + scene boards → guide audio (verbatim read) → render (funding-gated) → QC preview → publish. A stage is not complete on a partial check: duration alone is not audio QC; a thumbnail preview at small size is not a text verification (check the standalone file); a UI screen is not a working pipeline.
- HOW TO CREATE IT: At each stage, write the artifact into the goal's files, verify it (read it back, check it against its source), and report it before starting the next stage.
- REQUIRED RETURN: Per stage, the artifact itself plus its verification note (what was checked, against what).
- GATE: Any stage may be stopped by the user at approval points (script approval, lead confirmation, render go-ahead, publish). Unconfirmed coordinator picks (e.g. leads the outline did not name) are flagged as unconfirmed, never presented as decided.

## D13. Balance rule
- STATEMENT: The series shows the shadow side too — bad habits, thoughts, beliefs, and true hardships and their effects — not only the positive. Every shadow piece carries its redemption visibly: the cost is counted honestly, and the way back is shown, not just the fall.
- HOW TO CREATE IT: Build shadow episodes on the same spine as light ones (D4), with the cost accounting explicit (what waiting/habit/belief cost, versus what doing it costs) and the redemption beat concrete (what the sufferer learned, what they do now, what the wreckage becomes — e.g. dome panes become windows).
- REQUIRED RETURN: The cost accounting and the redemption beat, both present in the script, not implied.
- GATE: No shadow piece ships without its redemption; no light piece ships by pretending the shadow does not exist.

## D14. Origin and cosmology rule
- STATEMENT: Origin stories are grounded in the real, measured cosmic record (13.8-billion-year age; inflation; first light at ~380,000 years; first stars; Milky Way, Sun, Earth, and life timelines; our cosmic address out to Laniakea; accelerating expansion and the far-future thinning). The series' telling uses epic space-opera feel only — no borrowed franchise names, terms, or lore (see D3). Framing: there was nothing before, then everything all at once, and it became; the Originals are one entity that formed and became individual ("as they became, so did they"); becoming is evermore until the end of nothing left.
- HOW TO CREATE IT: Write the factual record first, then the mythic telling on top of it, keeping the two distinguishable. Place the origin as the first section of the series page.
- REQUIRED RETURN: The origin section + the factual record it stands on, with figures stated.
- GATE: No origin claim presented as measured fact when it is mythic telling, and no borrowed lore smuggled in as "feel."

## D15. No-assumption rule
- STATEMENT: Never assume an outcome the user has not confirmed: no assumed assessment, case number, call outcome, render, recording, or publish. Never re-flag a handled or paused item. Never merge accounts, counties, tracks, or characters on assumption. A correction propagates through every affected memory and deliverable, not just the reply.
- HOW TO CREATE IT: Mark every unconfirmed item as unconfirmed in the artifact itself. When corrected, reopen the saved outputs and fix them; do not leave outdated files standing.
- REQUIRED RETURN: An open-decisions list carried in every package, stating what is decided, what is not, and what is blocked.
- GATE: An unconfirmed item cannot be cited as fact in a later stage.

- GATE: No build is called "the system" without that match. No move, disconnect, or swap starts on a hold (see D2).
