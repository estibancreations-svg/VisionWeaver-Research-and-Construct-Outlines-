Script Draft - Verbatim

The script is the source - these words do not change in production

Speaker is Okta throughout, except the man's remembered lines inside the Story. She argues both sides fairly, refuses to choose for the student, and lets the calm test decide. Pauses after the question lines are part of the script - see the standards mapping.

Hook

"Before you decide, ask one question: is this fear speaking, or is this you?"

Story - a decision that could not wait

A man came to the river path with a decision that could not wait - or so he said. Two roads stood before him: leave the town that raised him for work across the water, or stay where his roots and his people were. He argued both sides himself, passionately, and both sounded true. So I did not choose for him. Choosing for a person is not teaching; it is theft with kind hands. Instead I asked him: "When did you last decide something well?" He thought, and then he remembered: never in a storm. Never in the first hour of fear.

Delivery note. The refusal is the teaching: she does not choose for him. Let "Choosing for a person is not teaching; it is theft with kind hands" land, then hold a full pause after the question, "When did you last decide something well?"

Script Draft - Verbatim, Continued

Both sides in full, then the question that matters

So we set the dilemma on the table, fairly, both sides in full. The case for leaving: growth, provision, a door that is open now and may not be open later. The case for staying: roots, care, a life already built and people who would feel the leaving. Neither side was a villain. Neither side was a lie. And then I asked the question that matters: which voice is calm? Because you will know good from bad when you are calm, at peace. Not because calm makes you wise - because fear makes everything loud, and loud is not the same as true. I gave him the calm test: wait, breathe, walk the river path, sleep if you can - then listen for the voice that is still there when the noise dies down. That voice is yours. The loud one was only visiting.

Delivery notes for this section.
Give each case its full weight - neither side is a villain, neither side is a lie.
One soft bell tone sounds at the calm-test moment, and at no other point in this section.
"That voice is yours. The loud one was only visiting." lands as two short lines, each followed by a pause.

Script Draft - Verbatim, Continued

Discernment is a state you return to

Reframe

Hear this, and hold it. Discernment is not a skill you use in the storm. It is a state you return to before you use any skill. Fear speaks first and fastest; wisdom speaks second and quieter. Waiting is not weakness. Waiting is how you hear the second voice. The person who decides in the storm is not brave - they are only loud, and loudness passes for courage only until the weather clears.

Delivery note. "Discernment is not a skill you use in the storm. It is a state you return to before you use any skill" is the centre of the episode. Hold a full pause after it, and after "Waiting is how you hear the second voice."

Script Draft - Verbatim, Continued

No decision inside the first hour of fear

Practice

So here is the practice, and it is a rule, not a suggestion. No irreversible decision inside the first hour of fear. Write the decision down. Wait the hour. Walk. Then read it back in your own calm voice. If it still holds when the noise is gone, it is yours. If it does not, it never was - it was only the storm, talking through your mouth.

Payoff / Bridge

Discernment is a state before it is a skill. And the hardest test of that state is not a choice about a road. It is a choice about a goodbye - what to hold, what to release, and how to love what is gone without gripping what remains. That is next week's teaching, and the last of this season: how to mourn the person and release the season.

Where this sits in the episode. End of script draft. The payoff sits at 70-85% of runtime in the scene map, and the bridge - not a hard goodbye - carries the viewer to Speech 8, "Mourn the Person. Release the Season."
