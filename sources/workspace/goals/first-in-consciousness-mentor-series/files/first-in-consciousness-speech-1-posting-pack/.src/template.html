Description — copy exactly

Stop measuring your life from the bank you left.

She came to Okta at the crossing with her pack half undone and her breath gone — not from the river, but from the watching. That one crossed in a morning. That one has already built a house on the far shore. She had been standing in the middle of her own life, doing the bravest part of it, and calling it behind.

Here is the reframe Okta gives her: nobody crosses from the bank. The bank is where the crossing was decided; it is not where it is done. Comparison takes your middle — your real, wet, working middle — and lays it against somebody else’s far shore, and calls the distance failure. But their shore was never your measurement. Mid-river is not behind. Mid-river is the address of everyone who ever crossed.

The practice — today: one task, done at river pace. No clock on it. No second task riding on its back. No measuring it against how fast it went for anyone else. Feet in the water, one task, whole attention. When the old voice says behind, answer it with your feet: mid-river.

Peace is a pace, not a place. A pace can be chosen here — mid-river, mid-life, mid-everything — today.

Next week: Speech 2 — “Hurry Is Fear With Its Shoes On.” Why does hurry feel so much like safety? Hurry has a reason, and the reason has a name.
