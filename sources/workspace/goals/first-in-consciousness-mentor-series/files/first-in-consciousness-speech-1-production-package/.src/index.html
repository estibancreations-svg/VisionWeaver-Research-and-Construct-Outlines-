Approved script — verbatim · Approved 22 Oct 2026

The script is the source — these words do not change in production

Speaker is Okta throughout, except the traveller’s quoted lines inside the Story. Pauses after punch lines are part of the script — see the standards mapping.

Hook

“Stop measuring your life from the bank you left.”

Story

She came to me at the crossing, this traveller, with her pack half undone and her breath gone. Not from the river. From the watching.

“That one crossed in a morning,” she said. “That one has already built a house on the far shore. I have been in this water — how long have I been in this water?”

I have walked a long time. One ring on my staff for every hundred years, and the bell has heard this speech before — from kings, from children, from my own mouth, once, when I was young enough to hurry.

So I did not answer her question. I asked her one.

“Where are your feet?”

“In the river,” she said, as if I were simple.

“And where does a crossing happen,” I said, “from the bank — or from the feet?”

She laughed then, a wet, tired laugh. Because she knew. She had been standing in the middle of her own life, doing the bravest part of it, and calling it behind — because she was measuring from a shore she was no longer standing on.

Approved script — verbatim, continued

Reframe — nobody crosses from the bank

Hear me. Nobody crosses from the bank.

The bank is where the crossing was decided. It is not where it is done. The crossing is done here — in the cold part, in the slow part, in the part where the far shore looks no closer than it did yesterday.

Comparison is a trick of measurement. It takes your middle — your real, wet, working middle — and lays it against somebody else’s far shore, and calls the distance failure. But their shore is not your measurement. It never was. You did not see their middle. You did not stand in their water.

Mid-river, you are — not behind. Mid-river is not a falling-short. Mid-river is the address of everyone who ever crossed.

The Current does not carry the ones who left. It carries the ones still standing in it.

Delivery notes for this section

  • “Mid-river, you are — not behind” is the episode’s punch line. Hold a full pause after it.
  • Keep the pace slow and even. The reframe lands by accumulation, not volume.
  • On-screen text appears only here and at the payoff — do not caption every line as text.

Practice and payoff — one task, at river pace

Practice

So today, one thing. One task — you choose it — done at river pace.

No clock on it. No second task riding on its back. No measuring it against how fast it went for anyone else. Feet in the water, one task, whole attention, river pace. When the old voice says behind, you answer it with your feet: mid-river — and you keep standing in the doing.

One task. That is all. The river does the rest, the way it has always done it: under you, around you, carrying what you cannot carry, for exactly as long as the crossing takes.

Payoff / Bridge

Peace is not the far shore. Peace was never waiting for you at the end of the crossing, holding a dry towel and a certificate.

Peace is a pace. Not a place. And a pace can be chosen here — mid-river, mid-life, mid-everything — today.

But I will tell you what she asked me next, this traveller, drying her feet on my shore. She said: “Then why does hurry feel so much like safety?”

Ah. That — that is next week’s teaching. Hurry has a reason, and the reason has a name.

Walk at river pace until then.

End of approved script. The payoff sits at 70–85% of runtime in the scene map, and the bridge — not a hard goodbye — carries the viewer to Speech 2, “Hurry Is Fear With Its Shoes On.”
