Script Draft - Verbatim

The script is the source - these words do not change in production

Speaker is Okta throughout, except the woman's remembered lines inside the Story. She listens first, reflects back, and reframes with a question. She never decides for the student. Pauses after the question lines are part of the script - see the standards mapping.

Hook

"Grief is not a problem to solve. It is a season to sit through — and seasons, by nature, end."

Story - the coat

A woman came to the riverbank carrying a coat — her mother's coat, worn at the elbows, still smelling faintly of her kitchen. She had kept it three years in a closet she could not open without crying, and she was ashamed twice over: ashamed of grieving so long, and ashamed of wanting to let go. I did not take the coat. I did not tell her to keep it or to give it away. I listened, the way the river listens — without hurrying it anywhere.

Delivery note. The listening is the teaching: she does not take the coat, and she does not tell her to keep it or to give it away. Hold Okta's listening stillness through this section, and let "without hurrying it anywhere" land before the question.

Script Draft - Verbatim, Continued

What season did this coat belong to?

Then I asked her: "What season did this coat belong to?" And she remembered: winter mornings, soup on the stove, her mother at the door waving her off into the cold. I said: "Then thank it for that season. The coat did its work. The person is not the coat — the person is the winter mornings you carry, and those you keep without a closet." She held the coat, thanked it aloud, and decided — herself — to give it to someone cold this winter, keeping one button in a small box. Not because I told her. Because she was ready, and readiness is a decision only the grieving can make.

Delivery notes for this section.
Hold a long pause after the question, "What season did this coat belong to?"
One soft bell tone sounds at the thanking moment, and at no other point in this section.
"Not because I told her. Because she was ready" lands as two short lines. The decision is hers - Okta never decides for her.

Script Draft - Verbatim, Continued

Mourn the person. Release the season.

Reframe

Hear this, gently. You do not let go of the person. You let go of the season the person lived in, so the love can live in a new one. Mourning the person and releasing the season are two different acts, and you can do one without betraying the other. Death is natural; twilight comes to every season, not as punishment but as the turning of the Current. Grief that will not move is love with nowhere to go — give it somewhere to go. Give the coat to someone cold. Give the story to someone young. Give the love a new season to live in.

Delivery note. "You do not let go of the person. You let go of the season the person lived in" is the centre of the episode. Speak it gently, hold a full pause after it, and let each "Give..." line arrive separately: the coat, the story, the love.

Script Draft - Verbatim, Continued

Keep the love - and the season closes

Practice

So here is the practice, and it is yours to choose, not mine. Thank one object, one photo, or one place for its season — aloud, by name — and then keep it or release it in love. Either choice is faithful. The only unfaithful choice is the one you make from shame, instead of from thanks.

Payoff / Bridge

This is where our first season closes. Walk with me back over the road we have walked together: you learned to walk at river pace, to name your fear, to hold the hand without gripping the riverbank, to sit with your failures until they taught you, to step in fully, to define your terms, and to know in calm. And now you know how to let a season end without letting the love end. Season 2 opens with unlearning — because everything you have learned, you will have to learn again, lightly, the way the river learns its banks new every spring. Until then: mourn the person. Release the season. Keep the love.

Where this sits in the episode. End of script draft. The payoff sits at 70-85% of runtime in the scene map, and the bridge is a season-close farewell that opens Season 2 - never a hard goodbye, and not a final goodbye.
