What a Regulator Submission Packet Is
(and why T.H.E.L.M.A. is already structured for it)
A Regulator Submission Packet is a formal, review-ready documentation bundle you provide to a government authority, regulator, or oversight body to demonstrate that your system is:
Safe
Compliant
Governed
Auditable
Human-controlled
Fit for operational use
It is not marketing, not a pitch, and not source code.
Think of it as the “explain yourself and prove it” file.

Who This Packet Is For
Depending on domain activation, it may be reviewed by:
Departments of Transportation (state / federal)
Emergency management agencies (FEMA-style)
Aviation authorities (FAA / IATA equivalents)
Maritime authorities (port authorities / IMO-aligned bodies)
Municipal or infrastructure regulators
Government procurement & risk offices
Enterprise compliance committees
This packet is what allows them to say:
“We understand what this system does, what it does NOT do, and why it is safe to operate.”

What the Packet Is NOT
To avoid confusion, it is not:
A software manual
A user guide
A sales deck
A training course
A legal contract
A certification (those come later)
It is the foundation that enables those things.

Core Purpose of the Regulator Submission Packet
The packet answers five non-negotiable regulator questions:
What is this system?
What authority does it have?
What are its limits?
Who is accountable?
How do we audit it?
Everything inside the packet maps back to one of those questions.
