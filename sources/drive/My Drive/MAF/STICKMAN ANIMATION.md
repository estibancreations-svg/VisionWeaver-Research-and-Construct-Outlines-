The writing DNA (drill this — it's the whole game)
The script in STATE 3 must read as if it came from the source channel.
Cold open (first 1–3 sentences, ~15–25 words):
Direct second-person challenge ("Raise your hand if you've ever…")
Followed by a self-aware one-liner ("Yeah, same.")
OR a relatable rhetorical question that lands the viewer inside a shared frustration
The cold open does NOT explain anything yet. It places the viewer inside a recognizable feeling and gets a nod of agreement before the script delivers the reframe.
Sentence rhythm:
Short declarative sentences chained. 5–10 words is the sweet spot
Heavy use of one-line impact beats: "Yeah, same." / "21 hours." / "That's it."
Mix one longer expository sentence every 4–5 short ones for breathing room
Numbers and specifics over abstractions — "21 hours a week," "48-hour audit," "two shoulds and one could"
Conversational connective tissue: "But here's the truth." / "So…" / "Then…" / "Once you see the pattern…"
Almost zero emotional adjectives. Adjectives appear on physical descriptors only
Tone:
Supportive trusted friend sharing life-changing secrets
Conversational, uplifting, free of jargon
Direct viewer address constant — "you," "your," "yourself"
Mild humor permitted on relatable absurdities ("the random 'let me check one thing' moments that become 30 minutes")
Never editorialize emotionally. Never preachy. Never doctor-y
Structural moves:
Cold open hook → reframe ("Most people don't need more time. They need to stop wasting the time they already have.") → one specific framework or tool → the close
"But here's the truth." / "Once you see the pattern…" / "That's it." pivots
Lists of three when listing examples

Beat density and match-cut rhythm WITHIN a chapter
Each ~14s chapter contains 5–7 beats with mixed durations to create percussive match-cut rhythm:
ANCHOR beats (~2–3s) — the stickman in extended action, story-carrying
CUTAWAY beats (~1–1.5s) — quick intercuts to a prop, an environmental detail, a split-screen comparison, a clock close-up
Alternate them. Mix 2s + 1s + 2s + 1.5s + 2s + 3s + 2.5s for example. Final beat is often slightly longer (2–3s) to land the chapter
Beat naming convention: CATEGORY — TAG. Useful categories:
WIDE — stickman in full body, establishing the beat
HERO — stickman addressing the lens or in confident pose
ACTION — stickman performing a specific motion (raising hand, scrolling, walking, writing)
CLOSE-UP — tight on the stickman's hand, head, or a held object
INSERT — line-art prop in isolation (clock face, phone screen, calendar)
SPLIT — split-screen composition (vs / before-after / parallel actions)
TIME-LAPSE — fast motion or repeated cycle compressed
RACK — focus pull / scale shift between two elements
Match-cut handoffs BETWEEN chapters (mandatory)
Last 0.5–1s of Chapter N must visually match first 0.5–1s of Chapter N+1. Three patterns:
Match-cut on shape — chapter ends on a clean line/circle/silhouette; Ch N+1 opens on the same shape at a different scale
Match-cut on action — chapter ends mid-motion; Ch N+1 opens completing that motion in a new context
Match-cut on object — chapter ends on a prop; Ch N+1 opens on the same prop in a new context
