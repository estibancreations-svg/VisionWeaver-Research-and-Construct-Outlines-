# Chapter 4: The Test

*May 2024*

Jordan Chen adjusted their blue-purple hair in the rearview mirror of Marcus's car, a nervous habit that had persisted since their college days. At twenty-eight, they had grown more comfortable in their non-binary identity, but certain situations still triggered the old anxieties—the feeling of being scrutinized, evaluated, found wanting in some fundamental way.

Meeting the woman who claimed to be Elijah's daughter definitely qualified as one of those situations.

"You okay?" Marcus asked from the driver's seat, his lawyer's perception catching the subtle signs of Jordan's discomfort.

"Just processing," Jordan replied, offering a small smile. "It's a lot, you know? The idea that Elijah had this whole other life, this child he never mentioned. Makes me wonder what else we didn't know about him."

Marcus nodded, his eyes on the road as they approached the Bayshore Café. "People contain multitudes. Especially people who lived through the times Elijah did, when compartmentalization was often a survival strategy."

The observation resonated with Jordan's own experience. They had spent years compartmentalizing different aspects of their identity—Chinese American and non-binary, academic and activist, private self and public persona. The integration they had achieved over the past few years had been hard-won, facilitated in large part by the safe house community and the mentorship Elijah had provided before his death.

"I brought something that might be relevant," Jordan said, patting the messenger bag on their lap. "I found it when we were cleaning out Elijah's personal effects after the will reading."

Marcus glanced over, curiosity evident in his expression. "What is it?"

"A wooden box with letters from someone named Grace—Katherine's mother, if her story checks out. They're... painful. Rejections of Elijah's attempts to be involved in his child's life."

Marcus's hands tightened slightly on the steering wheel, the only visible sign of his emotional response. "You've had these for two years? Why didn't you mention them before?"

The question wasn't accusatory, but Jordan felt a twinge of defensiveness nonetheless. "They seemed so private. And with everything happening—the inheritance, the renovation, all the safe house programs we were developing—I wasn't sure what to do with them. They didn't seem immediately relevant until now."

Marcus nodded, accepting this explanation. "Fair enough. They could be important evidence supporting Katherine's claim, beyond the DNA test."

"That's what Victor said when I showed him this morning," Jordan agreed. "If she turns out to be Elijah's daughter, these letters are part of her history too."

They fell silent as Marcus navigated the final turns to the café, each lost in their own thoughts about the meeting ahead. Jordan found themselves wondering how Katherine Johnson might react to them—to their non-binary presentation, their role in the safe house community, their connection to Elijah. Would she see them as family of a sort, people who had shared her father's life in ways she hadn't been able to? Or as strangers who knew a man she was only beginning to learn about?

The Bayshore Café was moderately busy with the afternoon crowd when they arrived, but Marcus had reserved a corner table that offered some privacy. Victor and Desiree were already waiting in the foyer, Victor clutching an archival folder that suggested he had brought his own historical materials to the meeting.

"Ready?" Marcus asked, his gaze taking in each of them in turn.

Jordan nodded, though "ready" seemed an inadequate description for the complex emotions they were experiencing. They followed Marcus into the café, scanning the room automatically for potential threats—a habit developed during years of navigating public spaces as a visibly gender-nonconforming person.

Marcus led them to the corner table, where they arranged themselves with the practiced coordination of people who had worked closely together for years. Jordan positioned themselves with a clear view of the entrance, wanting to observe Katherine Johnson's arrival, to form their first impressions without the pressure of immediate interaction.

When she entered a few minutes later, Jordan felt an immediate jolt of recognition. It wasn't just the physical resemblance to Elijah—though that was striking, particularly around the eyes—but something in her bearing, the dignified way she carried herself that echoed the man who had been so important in Jordan's life.

Marcus made the introductions, and Jordan noted Katherine's direct gaze as she greeted each of them in turn. There was no hesitation when she shook Jordan's hand, no flicker of discomfort at their gender presentation—just the same respectful attention she had offered to Marcus and Desiree.

"Thank you all for meeting with me," Katherine began once they were seated. "I realize this must be unexpected and potentially unsettling."

"Perhaps you could tell us how you came to believe Elijah was your father," Marcus suggested gently. "Your letter mentioned learning this after your mother's death."

Katherine nodded, taking a sip of water before beginning her story. "My mother, Grace Johnson, never told me who my father was. Growing up, whenever I asked, she would say he wasn't part of our lives and it was better not to discuss him. As a child, I imagined all sorts of scenarios—that he was famous, or in prison, or had died heroically."

She smiled faintly at the memory before continuing. "Two years ago, my mother was diagnosed with pancreatic cancer. It progressed quickly. Near the end, when she knew she didn't have much time left, she asked my aunt—her sister—to tell me the truth after she was gone."

Katherine paused, collecting herself. "My aunt waited until after the funeral. She told me that my mother had been married briefly in the mid-1970s to a man named Elijah Johnson. According to my aunt, the marriage ended when my mother discovered Elijah was bisexual—though my aunt used less accepting terminology."

Jordan felt a pang of recognition at this familiar pattern of rejection and exposure. Their own parents had initially reacted with similar shock and disapproval when they came out as non-binary, though their relationship had gradually evolved toward acceptance over the years.

"My mother was deeply religious and from a conservative family," Katherine continued. "When she discovered Elijah had been with men before their marriage, she felt betrayed. According to my aunt, she exposed him to everyone in their community—family, church, neighbors. It created a scandal that forced him to leave town."

The revelation landed heavily among them. Jordan's expression darkened with recognition of the familiar pattern of rejection and exposure that had driven so many to seek sanctuary at the safe house over the decades.

"My mother was already pregnant with me when they separated," Katherine continued. "She returned to her family, changed back to her maiden name, and raised me as a single mother. She never told me about Elijah, and according to my aunt, she rejected all his attempts to make contact or provide support."

"He tried to reach you?" Jordan asked, their voice carefully neutral despite the emotion rising in their chest.

Katherine nodded. "According to my aunt, yes. For several years after I was born, he sent letters, cards, even child support checks that my mother returned uncashed. Eventually, he stopped trying—or perhaps he moved away. My aunt didn't know the details."

"He moved to Tampa in 1980," Victor supplied quietly. "He never mentioned having a child, but as I said, he rarely spoke about that period of his life. It was clearly painful for him."

As Katherine continued sharing her story—about her research into Elijah's life after learning his identity, about her decision to contact them—Jordan found themselves studying her more closely. There was a thoughtfulness to her manner, a careful consideration before speaking that reminded them of Elijah. She seemed genuinely interested in understanding her father rather than making claims on his legacy, her questions focused on who he had been rather than what he had owned.

When Marcus brought out the DNA testing materials, Jordan reached for their messenger bag, exchanging a glance with Victor. After Victor shared the photograph he had discovered in the archives—which Katherine confirmed showed her mother with Elijah—Jordan felt it was time to reveal what they had brought.

"We found this among Elijah's personal effects," Jordan explained, placing the cloth-wrapped wooden box on the table. "It contains items that appear to be related to your mother and possibly to you as an infant."

Katherine's eyes widened as Jordan unwrapped the cloth, revealing the intricately carved box. "I recognize this pattern," she said softly. "My aunt had a similar box that she said came from Nigeria."

With careful hands, Jordan opened the box, revealing its contents—the wedding ring, the lock of hair, the tiny shoe, the folded letters. Katherine's composure wavered visibly as she took in these tangible connections to her father and her own infancy.

"May I?" she asked, reaching toward the box.

"Of course," Victor said. "If you are indeed Elijah's daughter, these items are part of your history as much as his."

Jordan watched as Katherine examined each item with evident emotion—the tiny shoe she identified as her own, the lock of hair her mother had apparently sent to Elijah, the wedding ring she studied with particular intensity. When she turned to the letters, Jordan felt a renewed sense of the pain these documents contained.

"These are from my mother to Elijah?" Katherine asked.

"Yes," Jordan confirmed. "They span about a year, from 1976 to 1977. They're... difficult reading. Your mother was very clear about not wanting Elijah in your life."

As Katherine read the first letter, her expression growing more pained with each line, Jordan felt a complex mixture of emotions—sympathy for this woman discovering painful truths about her parents, sadness for Elijah and the relationship with his daughter he had been denied, anger at the injustice of a society that had made such separation seem necessary or justified.

"She was so angry," Katherine said finally, carefully refolding the letter. "So certain she was protecting me from something harmful." She looked up, meeting Victor's gaze directly. "Did he talk about this at all? About having a child he wasn't allowed to know?"

As Victor explained that Elijah had never explicitly mentioned having a child, Jordan reflected on their own relationship with the man who had become a mentor and father figure to them. Elijah had created space for Jordan to explore their gender identity without judgment, had offered guidance without imposing expectations, had modeled a kind of integrated authenticity that had profoundly influenced Jordan's own journey.

The possibility that he had been denied the opportunity to be that kind of father to his biological child added another layer of poignancy to his legacy. The safe house itself—a sanctuary for those rejected by family and society—took on new meaning in light of Elijah's personal experience of rejection and separation.

"I have a son," Katherine said, reaching for her phone and pulling up a photograph. "Alex is nineteen now. He's transgender—began his transition in high school." She showed them the photo—a handsome young man with a bright smile, clearly comfortable in his own skin. "When he first came out, I educated myself about LGBTQ+ issues, trying to be the supportive parent he needed. That's when I started noticing stories about the safe house in local news."

The connection—her son's identity leading her to become aware of the community her father had helped build, years before she knew of her connection to him—struck Jordan as profoundly meaningful. It suggested a kind of continuity across generations, a thread connecting Elijah to the grandson he had never known.

"After learning about Elijah, I wondered if there might be some genetic component to Alex's gender identity—some inherited trait that connected him to the grandfather he never knew," Katherine continued. "It's probably not that simple, but it made me feel like there might be a purpose to finally discovering this connection."

Jordan felt a surge of emotion at this revelation. As someone who had struggled with their own gender identity, they had often wondered about the interplay of biology, psychology, and social context in shaping such fundamental aspects of self. The idea that Alex's journey might connect in some way to Elijah's legacy created a sense of continuity that transcended the separation imposed by circumstance.

"I'd love to meet him sometime," Jordan said, surprising themselves with the spontaneous offer. "If that's something you and he would be comfortable with. We have a youth program at the safe house that includes several transgender teens. And I teach a course on gender studies at the university that explores these questions of identity formation across generations."

Katherine's expression warmed. "I think he'd like that. Alex has been curious about his grandfather since I told him what my aunt revealed. And he's always looking to connect with other transgender people, especially those with different generational perspectives."

The conversation continued, with Marcus explaining the DNA testing process and arrangements being made for future contact. Jordan found themselves increasingly drawn to Katherine—not just because of her connection to Elijah but because of her evident thoughtfulness, her support of her transgender son, her openness to exploring this new dimension of her identity without imposing expectations or demands.

When they parted, the goodbye was warmer than their initial greeting—not quite embraces, but containing the possibility of deeper connection in the future. Katherine carefully returned the letters to the wooden box but asked if she might keep it and its contents, a request they readily granted.

"Regardless of what the DNA test shows," she told them, "learning about Elijah, about the community he built and the lives he touched—it's already given me something valuable. A connection to a part of myself I didn't know was missing."

Driving back to the safe house, Jordan sat quietly in the passenger seat, processing the meeting and its implications. The others respected their silence, understanding that each of them needed space to integrate this new information into their understanding of Elijah and their own connection to his legacy.

Back at the safe house, as the others dispersed to their various responsibilities, Jordan found themselves drawn to the garden—a space they had transformed from overgrown neglect into a thriving community resource, with vegetable beds, medicinal herbs, and native plants that supported local pollinators. Working with plants had become a form of meditation for Jordan, a way of processing complex emotions through physical engagement with the natural world.

As they knelt in the soil, weeding around the young tomato plants, Jordan reflected on the meeting with Katherine and what her existence might mean for their understanding of Elijah. If the DNA test confirmed her claim, she represented not just a biological connection to Elijah but a continuation of his legacy in unexpected forms—through her work as a nurse, through her support of her transgender son, through her evident commitment to understanding and reconciliation.

The possibility of meeting Alex particularly intrigued Jordan. As someone who had navigated their own gender journey without family support initially, they had become passionate about creating resources for younger LGBTQ+ people facing similar challenges. The safe house's youth programs, the gender studies courses Jordan taught at the university, the performance workshops they led in community spaces—all were expressions of this commitment to supporting authentic self-expression across generations.

The idea that Elijah's grandson might benefit from these resources, might find connection to his grandfather's legacy through the community Elijah had built, created a sense of continuity that transcended the separation imposed by circumstance. It suggested that even when biological relationships were disrupted, other forms of kinship could emerge—connections based on shared experience, mutual recognition, and the creation of space for authentic self-expression.

Jordan was so absorbed in these reflections that they didn't notice Marcus approaching until he was standing beside the garden bed.

"Thought I might find you here," he said, offering a glass of iced tea. "You always head to the garden when you're processing something significant."

Jordan accepted the tea gratefully, brushing soil from their hands. "Just thinking about Katherine. And Alex. The idea that Elijah has a grandson he never knew, who's transgender... it feels meaningful somehow. Like his legacy continues in ways none of us could have anticipated."

Marcus nodded, taking a seat on the garden bench nearby. "I've been thinking about that too. About how our understanding of family has evolved over the past few years—from legal definitions to chosen connections to these unexpected biological revelations."

The observation resonated with Jordan's own journey. When they had first arrived at the safe house as a college student seeking community, their understanding of family had been shaped primarily by their difficult relationship with their parents. Elijah had introduced a different model—family as chosen connection, as mutual recognition and support, as space where authentic self-expression was valued rather than suppressed.

"Do you think the DNA test will confirm it?" Jordan asked. "That Katherine is really Elijah's daughter?"

Marcus considered the question with his characteristic thoughtfulness. "The physical resemblance is striking. And the timeline aligns with what Victor remembers from that period of Elijah's life. But we'll have scientific verification soon enough. The testing service has agreed to expedite the analysis."

They fell silent for a moment, each lost in their own thoughts about what this verification might mean for their understanding of Elijah and their own connection to his legacy.

"I should get back to work," Marcus said finally, rising from the bench. "I have a consultation with a transgender youth seeking emancipation from unsupportive parents. Cases like that always feel especially important now that we understand more about Elijah's own experience of family separation."

After Marcus left, Jordan returned to their gardening, finding comfort in the rhythmic work of tending growing things. The safe house garden had become a metaphor for their approach to community building—creating conditions where diverse elements could thrive together, where growth emerged from proper care and attention, where beauty and nourishment coexisted in mutually reinforcing ways.

"Taking her evening nap," Gloria replied with the pride of a devoted grandmother. "That child has lungs, let me tell you. Gets that from her mother—Desiree was the same way as a baby, singing before she could talk."

The casual reference to intergenerational traits reminded Jordan of Katherine's wondering about genetic connections between Elijah and her transgender son. While gender identity was far more complex than simple genetic inheritance, the idea that certain predispositions might be passed down through families wasn't entirely without foundation.

Jordan's own parents had struggled initially with their non-binary identity, unable to find precedent for it within their understanding of Chinese family traditions. It had taken years of patient education, of finding examples of gender diversity within Chinese history and culture, before they began to move toward acceptance. Now, their relationship had evolved to the point where Jordan's mother had recently asked thoughtful questions about pronouns and their father had defended Jordan's identity to a conservative relative.

He had helped Jordan navigate the complex terrain of family reconciliation, offering guidance based on his own experience of cultural navigation as a Nigerian immigrant in America.
